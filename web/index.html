A Spatial-Temporal Data Storytelling Platform for Hong Kong Air Pollution

This project explores three research stories: tropical cyclones and haze, traffic-related NO2, and seasonal pollution patterns from 2020–2025.

Story 1 — Tropical Cyclone Warning Signals and Air Quality

How does Hong Kong's air quality vary during different tropical cyclone warning-signal periods, and do the patterns differ across monitoring stations?

A tropical cyclone warning signal is an alert issued by the Hong Kong Observatory (HKO) when a tropical cyclone threatens Hong Kong. This story groups Signals 1 and 3 as lower signals, and Signals 8, 9 and 10 as higher signals. It compares hourly readings from 18 monitoring stations (2020–2025) for four pollutants: NO2 (nitrogen dioxide), O3 (ozone), RSP (respirable suspended particulates) and FSP (fine suspended particulates). All concentrations are in µg/m³.

Observational data only. Patterns are associations and do not show that cyclones caused any change in pollution.

1. Warning-signal timeline

The panel on the right shows each HKO warning-signal interval, grouped into 44 events. Labels and timestamps are kept as recorded in the HKO workbook. Super Typhoon Doksuri (E20) had Signal 1 only (26 July 2023 20:40 to 28 July 2023 12:40), so it cannot be used as a lower-versus-higher comparison.

2. Lower versus higher signals

A median is the middle value of a station's hourly readings. Each box below summarises one median per event and station, using only units that meet the 75% completeness rule (at least 75% of eligible hours have valid values).

3. Paired comparison within events

A pair compares the higher-signal median with the lower-signal median for the same event, station and pollutant. Positive values mean the higher-signal median was greater. Pairs are not independent across stations or hours.

4. Before, during and after one event

The before and after windows are 24 hours long and sit next to the signal period. Each window needs at least 12 free hours, meaning hours that are not inside a warning-signal interval or shared with another window. During-signal boxes use pure hourly bins only, so transition hours are excluded.

5. Sensitivity and limitations

The primary result is the 24-hour analysis. For 48 hours, the full-window-only comparison is the main sensitivity check. The all-events 48-hour row is transparency only and is not the main sensitivity result.

Story 2 — Daily NO₂ Rhythm

At what times of day is NO₂ highest, and does the daily pattern look different on weekdays and weekends?

Nitrogen dioxide (NO₂) is a gas linked to fuel combustion, including vehicle exhaust. This story uses hourly NO₂ records from the 18 monitoring stations in the project dataset (2020–2025). Each station's readings are first averaged for each hour and day type, then combined across stations.

The solid line is the median across stations: the middle value, which is less affected by one unusual station. The shaded band covers the 25th to 75th percentile range across stations. A station-hour is used only when at least 50% of its expected days have a valid reading. Missing readings are left out, never counted as zero.

Story 2 — Weekday Minus Weekend, Hour by Hour

Is the weekday–weekend difference the same at every hour, or does it change through the day?

For each station and hour, the weekend average is subtracted from the weekday average, so a positive value means weekdays were higher. The line is the median of these differences across stations that qualify on both day types, and the band is the 25th–75th percentile range.

This is a descriptive comparison. Weekdays and weekends differ in many ways, so the difference is not attributed to any single cause.

Story 2 — Station Comparison

Do stations show similar weekday and weekend averages, and which stations need cautious reading?

Each bar is a station's average across its qualifying hours on each day type. Stations are sorted by weekday average. The table shows how many valid observations support each average. No station has been removed or merged.

Story 2 — Limitations and What This Does Not Show

How far can these patterns be trusted, and what can they not tell us?

  • This is observational data. The patterns are associations and do not show that traffic or any other factor causes the NO₂ levels.
  • No traffic data is used. There is no verified mapping between traffic detectors and monitoring stations, so no traffic link is made.
  • Weekday and weekend are calendar days (Monday–Friday and Saturday–Sunday). Public holidays are not adjusted for.
  • Hour labels follow the recorded timestamps. The EPD hour convention and the HKT timezone are working assumptions, not confirmed source documentation.
  • NO₂ units are as recorded in the source file. These units have not been verified for this file.
  • Station coverage differs. NORTH and SOUTHERN weekday data start in July 2020, and TAP_MUN has unusually low values. CENTRAL and CENTRAL/WESTERN are kept as separate series and have not been verified as duplicates.

The counts in the panel are read from output/analysis/story2/story2_validation_summary.json.

Story 4 — Daily Maximum AQHI Categories

How does the distribution of daily maximum AQHI categories vary across Hong Kong monitoring stations, and how frequently do higher categories occur?

The Air Quality Health Index (AQHI) is a health-risk indicator related to air pollution. These charts count how many days each month had a given daily maximum AQHI category at a monitoring station. They are counts of days, not hourly duration and not the number of people affected. Change the station to explore spatial differences.

Key finding: days in category 7 or above (including 10+)

Chart A — Categories 1–5

The upper chart shows how often each lower daily maximum category occurred in each month. Lower categories do not mean there are no health effects.

Chart B — Categories 6–10 and 10+

The lower chart shows how often higher daily maximum categories occurred. 10+ is its own category and is never treated as the number 10.

Starred readings, if present, are drawn hatched. Missing, unresolved and unknown records are not plotted as AQHI categories. South District (南區) and North District (北區) are shown as not available before June 2020, not as zero. Station coverage varies, and these descriptive counts do not explain what caused the patterns.
